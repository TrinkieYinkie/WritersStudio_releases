# Volume II Outline

## Chapters 24–27 — Reassessment begins

Jane tries to interpret Bingley’s silence generously. Mrs Gardiner questions Wickham’s suitability and invites Elizabeth to travel. The visit to Charlotte at Hunsford moves Elizabeth into Darcy’s family orbit.

## Chapters 28–33 — Rosings pressure

Lady Catherine interrogates Elizabeth and performs rank as authority. Darcy visits the parsonage with increasing frequency. Colonel Fitzwilliam inadvertently reveals Darcy’s role in separating Bingley and Jane.

## Chapters 34–36 — Proposal, refusal, letter

Darcy proposes while emphasizing the obstacles he has overcome. Elizabeth refuses and charges him with harming Jane and Wickham. His letter supplies evidence that unsettles her confidence in both accusations.

## Chapters 37–42 — Changed knowledge, incomplete change

Elizabeth leaves Kent with a revised understanding but no expectation of seeing Darcy differently in action. Lydia goes to Brighton despite Elizabeth’s warning. The Gardiners’ tour creates the possibility of visiting Pemberley.

## Volume turn

The moral reversal is internal: Elizabeth can no longer trust the pleasure of being certain.
