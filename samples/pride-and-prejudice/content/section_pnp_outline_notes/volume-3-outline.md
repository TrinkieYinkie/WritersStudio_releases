# Volume III Outline

## Chapters 43–45 — Pemberley

The estate, its household testimony, and Darcy’s altered manners revise Elizabeth’s picture of him. Georgiana’s welcome suggests a future Elizabeth had never seriously imagined.

## Chapters 46–52 — Lydia crisis

News of Lydia’s elopement ends the visit. The family expects disgrace; Darcy quietly finds the couple and helps arrange the marriage. Elizabeth learns his role only after Lydia’s careless hint leads her to Mrs Gardiner’s letter.

## Chapters 53–57 — Returns and resistance

Bingley and Darcy return to Netherfield. Bingley proposes to Jane. Lady Catherine, alarmed by a rumour, demands that Elizabeth reject any future proposal from Darcy; Elizabeth refuses to surrender her freedom.

## Chapters 58–61 — Renewed proposals and resolution

Lady Catherine’s report gives Darcy hope. He and Elizabeth speak openly, become engaged, and secure family consent. The final chapter distributes the marriages into different households while preserving the relationships among them.

## Resolution

The ending rewards self-knowledge expressed through conduct, not merely corrected opinion.
