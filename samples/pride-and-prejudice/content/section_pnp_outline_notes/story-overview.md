# Story Overview

*Pride and Prejudice* follows Elizabeth Bennet as wit, attraction, social pressure, and incomplete evidence complicate her judgment of Fitzwilliam Darcy. Around their changing relationship, the novel contrasts several courtships and marriages to ask what affection needs from character, knowledge, money, and family.

## Central dramatic question

Can Elizabeth and Darcy learn enough about themselves—and each other—to choose a marriage based on respect rather than first impressions?

## Major movements

- **Attraction under resistance:** their attention sharpens while their interpretations remain hostile.
- **Crisis of judgment:** the first proposal and Darcy’s letter force both characters to confront their faults.
- **Changed conduct:** Pemberley and the Lydia crisis reveal transformation through action.
- **Renewed choice:** external opposition gives Elizabeth and Darcy the chance to speak with greater honesty.

## Parallel relationships

Jane and Bingley test the cost of modesty and persuasion. Charlotte and Collins expose the economics of security. Lydia and Wickham show attraction without judgment or responsibility.
